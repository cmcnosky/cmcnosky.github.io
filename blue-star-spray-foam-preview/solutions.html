HOW RECOMMENDATIONS ARE MADE

Open-cell or closed-cell
spray foam?

Open-cell and closed-cell spray foam behave differently. Blue Star reviews the full roof, wall, floor or crawlspace assembly before recommending a product, thickness or location.

OPEN-CELL AND CLOSED-CELL

Different tools for different conditions.

Exact performance comes from the selected product’s current technical data and the finished installation.

Planning comparison for open-cell and closed-cell spray foam
ConsiderationOpen-cellClosed-cell
StructureOpen-cellLower-density, softer foam with an open cellular structure.Closed-cellHigher-density, rigid foam with predominantly closed cells.
Available depthOpen-cellTypically needs more depth for the same thermal target.Closed-cellGenerally provides more thermal resistance where depth is limited.
Vapor behaviorOpen-cellMore vapor-permeable. The assembly may need a separate vapor-control strategy.Closed-cellLower vapor permeance at product-specific thicknesses. The complete assembly still needs review.
Liquid waterOpen-cellRoofing, flashing and drainage remain the liquid-water management system.Closed-cellMay resist water absorption more than open-cell products. Roofing, flashing and drainage remain the liquid-water management system.
Final selectionThe approved assembly, product documentation, available depth, fire protection, moisture strategy and project requirements determine the selection.

WHAT BLUE STAR REVIEWS

What Blue Star checks before recommending a system.

The review connects the proposed insulation to the actual building and project requirements.

  1. 01

    Where should the thermal and air-control layers go?

    Roofline, ceiling, wall, floor, crawlspace wall or another defined surface.

  2. 02

    How is the assembly built?

    Surface, framing, exterior layers, interior finish and nearby air or vapor controls.

  3. 03

    What conditions already exist?

    Leaks, damp materials, contamination, damaged surfaces or unresolved condensation.

  4. 04

    What performance is required?

    Adopted code or design target, available depth, continuity and approved product.

  5. 05

    Which protective covering applies?

    Thermal barrier, ignition protection or a product-specific tested assembly, according to location and project requirements.

  6. 06

    How will the work be executed?

    Access, occupied areas, isolation, ventilation, other trades, cleanup and product-specific re-entry.

Repair active water and damaged materials first. Installation starts with a suitable, clean and sufficiently dry surface that meets the selected product’s requirements.

INSTALLATION PLANNING

Air sealing changes how a building behaves.

Identify HVAC equipment, ducts, fuel-burning appliances and planned ventilation before work begins. The job plan should also cover work-area isolation, exhaust ventilation, surface protection, cleanup and the product-specific time for occupants and other trades to return.

MATERIAL QUESTIONS

Questions about spray foam.

Project and safety resources ↗
Can spray foam provide an air-control layer?+

Yes, when the selected product is installed at its tested thickness and detailed continuously. Connections at framing, edges, penetrations and transitions remain part of the work.

Is closed-cell foam a waterproofing system?+

Closed-cell insulation foam can resist water absorption. Roofing, flashing and drainage remain responsible for managing liquid water.

Can foam remain exposed?+

Spray polyurethane foam is combustible. Required thermal or ignition protection depends on the location, access, product evaluation report, tested installation and applicable requirements.

Who confirms the final assembly on a designed project?+

The current contract documents, responsible design professional, product documentation and applicable authority govern the final assembly. Blue Star can clarify its installation scope within that process.
