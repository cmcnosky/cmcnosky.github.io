AFTER A SUCCESSFUL SUBMISSION

What happens after you send a project.

The receipt screen confirms a successful submission and shows a project reference. Blue Star then decides what can be answered and what still needs to be verified.

01

Save the project reference.

The receipt screen confirms the submission and shows a reference. Copy or save it because the form does not automatically email you a receipt.

02

Blue Star reviews the source information.

Submitted details, files and links form the basis of the review. With your permission, a preliminary internal brief may help organize the material. Blue Star checks the source information and makes the final recommendation.

03

Resolve the important gaps.

Blue Star may follow up with a recommendation or ask for current documents, measurements, site conditions or schedule details needed to move forward.

04

Confirm the offer in writing.

Once the scope and conditions are understood, a written proposal can identify the price basis, included work, exclusions, assumptions and timing.

The receipt confirms the request, not the job. Submission does not create a contract, confirm bid participation, reserve an installation date or approve a product or design change. Call with the project reference when a deadline is close.

FORMAL BID PACKAGES

Send the package your team already uses.

The same project form can begin a commercial bid review. There is no separate product-selection questionnaire.

CURRENT DOCUMENTS

Identify the package to price.

Include the relevant plans, specifications, addenda, bid form, scope sheet and ordinary qualification requirements. State when a file is preliminary, incomplete or superseded.

DEADLINE & LOCATION

Make the timing unambiguous.

Provide the project city, bid deadline with time zone and expected installation window in the description. Call to confirm estimating availability before relying on a bid-day response.

ACCESS & CONTACT

Use files or an authorized link.

Upload the relevant PDF pages or provide a shared-file link Blue Star is authorized to open. Name the permission contact when access has to be arranged.

Keep restricted records in an appropriate channel. Do not place passwords, access codes, tax identifiers, financial statements, payment details, bank records or personal identifiers in the public form. Call before sending records that require a controlled plan room or nondisclosure agreement.
Issued project requirements still govern. Blue Star can flag a conflict, request clarification or describe an alternative for formal consideration. The review does not revise the contract documents or approve a substitution.

COMPARE WRITTEN QUOTES

Put every number on the same basis.

A useful comparison shows exactly what each contractor included and what could change the price.

01

Areas and quantity source

List the surfaces, measured area and source of the quantity. Separate base scope from alternates.

02

Product and performance basis

State the product, required thickness or performance target, assembly and document issue being priced.

03

Preparation and access

Identify removal, surface preparation, lifts, protection, occupied-area controls and work by other trades.

04

Price components

Separate mobilization, phasing, barriers or coatings, allowances, unit-price basis and taxes when they apply.

05

Assumptions and exclusions

Record open questions, site-review needs, excluded work and conditions that could change the number.

06

Timing and written terms

Confirm schedule, proposal validity, payment terms, warranty terms and the approval path for any alternative.

Keep the specified base scope visible. A proposed alternative follows the project’s design and approval process before it becomes part of the work.

BEFORE INSTALLATION

Prepare the building and the work plan.

The selected product and actual job conditions determine the final installation and safety plan.

  1. 01

    Confirm the scope and current documents.

    Identify the surfaces, approved assembly, product, thickness, protective covering and responsibilities carried by others.

  2. 02

    Correct water and substrate problems.

    Address active leaks, wet or damaged material, contamination and unsuitable surfaces before spraying.

  3. 03

    Clear access and protect adjacent property.

    Plan equipment access, heights, stored items, windows, doors, finishes and areas exposed to overspray.

  4. 04

    Coordinate HVAC, ignition sources and other trades.

    Identify air intakes, ducts, combustion appliances, occupied areas and anyone working near the controlled zone.

  5. 05

    Agree on isolation, ventilation and return timing.

    Use the selected product and job conditions to define access restrictions, exhaust, cleanup, re-entry and reoccupancy.

  6. 06

    Keep the product documents available.

    Technical data, safety data, evaluation information and project-required submittals should match the installed system.

PROJECT & SAFETY QUESTIONS

Questions about project review and installation.

Review system-selection factors ↗
Why is a site visit sometimes necessary?+

Plans and photos may omit surface condition, moisture, access, obstructions, occupied-area controls or the measurable spray area. A visit can resolve conditions that affect the recommendation, scope, price or schedule.

When will I know the ventilation and re-entry plan?+

Before installation. The selected product, amount of work, building layout, temperature, humidity and ventilation influence the job-specific plan.

What product documents should I expect?+

Once a system is selected, applicable technical data, safety data and code-evaluation information should be available. Commercial submittal and closeout requirements should follow the project documents.

How should I update a request after plans change?+

Email Joshua@bluestarsprayfoam.com or call (864) 373-4464 with the project reference and a short description of what changed. Blue Star can confirm how to provide the updated files.
